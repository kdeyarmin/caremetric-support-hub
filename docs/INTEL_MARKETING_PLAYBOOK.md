## 2. What CareMetric Intel is

CareMetric Intel runs a home health or hospice agency's whole marketing program in one workspace: deciding where to focus, finding
referral sources, working the relationships, and seeing what the work led to. Every message should leave the reader with that whole
picture, even when it leads with one part.

**The working loop the product supports:**

1. Research the territory: counties, ZIP areas, providers, facilities and competitors.
2. Build a focused list of referral sources, with a reason behind each name.
3. Put those accounts in a marketing CRM with an owner, contacts by role and a stage.
4. Plan the day, prepare each visit, log it from a phone and set a dated next step.
5. Record or import referrals and admissions, then review results with the team.

Expenses and mileage are logged along the way, as part of the field work. They are a small part of the workspace and never the point.

**What it is built on:** data from CMS, NPPES, the U.S. Census Bureau, HRSA, CDC, AHRQ, BLS, the HHS Office of Inspector General
and state licensing files, linked by NPI, CCN, county and ZIP. Every view shows which release it uses. These figures are research
signals; referrals are only what the agency records. Name the data by its source ("Medicare data", "CMS and Census data", "CMS
ratings"), never as public or free data, and never as proprietary, exclusive or licensed (owner direction 2026-11-17).

**Approved one-line pitch** (Intel's home page): "Know who to visit next. Make every follow-up count."

**The founder's story** is the asset no large competitor has. Tell it only as the fact sheet words it (`founder` in `facts.mjs`): in
outreach in the founder's first person, signed "Dr. Kevin C. Deyarmin, Founder, CareMetric"; in ads, posts and articles told about him.
Never add the agency's name, its results, any number or years, or a superlative.

## 3. Who buys and why now

The best-fit buyer is an independent or regional home health or hospice agency with 1 to 3 branches and about 3 to 10 users, where one
manager answers for referral growth. Hospitals, nursing homes and personal care homes can use the research tools on every plan, but
self-serve CRM workflows are home health and hospice only.

| Who | What they care about | Lead with |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Owner or administrator | Where a small team and its budget should go, how the competition is moving, what the marketing produced | Competitor intelligence; results reports |
| Director of business development or marketing | Territory plans, which sources to visit, follow-up discipline, recorded referrals | Referral-source finding; goals and team review; drop-off alerts |
| Marketer or community liaison | A focused visit list, less paperwork, notes and follow-ups in one place | Today page; pre-visit card; phone visit log; visit debrief |
| Growth or strategy lead | Where to send marketing time next, how markets compare | County intelligence; Site finder; market briefs |

**Buying triggers to look for:** a new marketer or liaison hire, a new director of business development, a slow quarter or a census
goal, a lost referral source, and annual planning. November through January is planning season, so planning angles land now.

**2026 market forces that shape every message:**

- **Growth means taking share.** CMS froze new Medicare enrollment of home health agencies and hospices on May 13, 2026, including new
  branches and some ownership changes. The freeze was set to run through November 13, 2026, and can be extended in six-month blocks. New
  agencies are rare, so most growth must come from winning referral sources in existing markets. Check the Federal Register or cms.gov
  before saying anything about the freeze's current status.
- **Hospitals are choosing partners on outcomes.** CMS's TEAM model began January 1, 2026, and participating hospitals answer for what
  happens after discharge. Agencies that can show response time and quality scores have a stronger story.
- **Hospice scores are now public.** The FY2027 hospice rule publishes each hospice's Service and Spending Variation Index (SSVI). It is
  an outlier index, not a quality rating, and must be described that way.
- **AI for field reps became normal in 2026.** Major vendors now market AI visit prep and note capture. Buyers will ask what Intel's AI
  does; describe the task, not the technology. CareMetric's AI is text only.
- **Fee-for-service data has a blind spot.** Most Medicare beneficiaries are now in Medicare Advantage, so Medicare fee-for-service
  figures tell part of the story. Say so plainly; it builds trust.

## 12. Guardrails checklist

Most items are already enforced in code; the rest depend on judgment.

**Always**

- Write plain, complete sentences under 30 words that a busy owner understands on the first read.
- Say something concrete: who it is for, and what they can do, see or stop doing.
- Show Intel as one marketing workspace, even when leading with one feature.
- Quote prices, the trial and the Founders Sale exactly as the fact sheet words them.
- Offer the trial in plain words. Do not bring up the credit card unless the reader asks what the trial needs or what happens when
  it ends (owner direction 2026-11-17); never say that no card is needed.
- Everywhere (email, replies, posts, ads, articles), name the data by its source ("Medicare data", "CMS and Census data", "CMS
  ratings") without the word "public" (owner direction 2026-11-17); code refuses it (`public_data`). Never call it proprietary,
  exclusive or licensed, and answer plainly when a reader asks where it comes from.
- Label Medicare and Census figures as research signals, and say referrals come from the agency's own records.
- Carry the plan condition when a feature needs one (Growth and Pro, Pro, agency admins and managers).
- Point to the trial, the walkthrough, the free county report or a written answer as the next step.
- Tag every product link with campaign tags; never tag citations or unsubscribe links.

**Never**

- Offer a demo, call, meeting or a time to talk.
- Name a competitor or an EHR vendor, or compare Intel with another product.
- State a result, testimonial, customer count, hours saved, return on investment or guarantee.
- Promise more referrals, revenue, compliance or a passed survey.
- Lead with expenses, the Stark limit or compliance, or call Intel a compliance tool.
- Lead with email campaigns or make email the subject.
- Claim a live EHR connection, an app-store app, claims-based referral flows or offline use.
- Repeat security or privacy certifications, or say the AI talks, listens or calls.
- Use the fact sheet's empty words: unlock, empower, leverage, seamless, robust, transform, elevate, solution, journey and the rest.
- Mention a real patient, resident or employee, or any health information.
- Say anything about the enrollment freeze, TEAM or a CMS rule without checking cms.gov or the Federal Register first.

## 14. What to measure

Judge the marketing by paid customers who stay, not by posts, clicks or drafts. Track each stage weekly in the Monday scorecard, by trial
cohort and with the dates and denominators shown:

1. Qualified prospects (fit score at the current minimum, with a permitted contact)
2. Messages delivered, and replies, split by lead pillar and angle
3. Free county reports requested and walkthrough visits, split by `utm_content`
4. Trials started, split by the ad, post or email tag they came from
5. First value: the trial's first real provider list
6. Activation: one list, five real accounts and a logged visit within seven days
7. Paid plans activated, by cohort
8. Customers still active after four weeks, and later at renewal

**Learning loop:** every Monday, compare reply and trial rates by pillar and by angle. Keep the two best, retire the weakest, and test one
new angle. Treat likes, clicks and generated assets as diagnostics only.
